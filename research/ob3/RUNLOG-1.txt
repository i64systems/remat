OB-3 asked: does choosing the resident set BY TASK collapse the
cross-domain miss penalty, with a deterministic detector making the
choice? Measured, on held-out bytes the ranking never saw:

  AC-CODE2, K=16, the SAME route log, only the resident set differs:

    SET-PROSE (OB-1's static prose-ranked set)   58.5814 pct miss
    SET-CODE  (detector-selected, this leg)      16.9796 pct miss
    SET-MIX   (pooled ranking, not switching)    24.4677 pct miss

  Region leasing cuts the held-out cross-domain miss from 58.5814 to
  16.9796 percent: a 41.6018 point absolute reduction, a 3.45x reduction
  in miss rate, decided by a frozen integer byte-classifier reading 4096
  bytes.

  At K=8 on the same held-out corpus: 79.0800 -> 40.0476 percent
  (SET-MIX 51.7148).

  SWITCHING BEATS MERGING, on held-out data as well as in simulation.
  SET-MIX is worse than SET-CODE on code at both K (24.4677 vs 16.9796 at
  K=16; 51.7148 vs 40.0476 at K=8). A static pooled compromise buys back
  much of the prose set's loss but never reaches what switching reaches.

  GENERALIZATION IS CHEAP HERE. The in-domain ceiling (r1, SET-CODE on
  the very corpus it was ranked on) is 14.4536 percent; the honest
  held-out figure (r3) is 16.9796 percent. The generalization gap is
  2.5260 points. SET-CODE does not merely memorize its ranking corpus.

  NO REGRESSION ON PROSE. r4 reproduces OB-1's own K=16 prose result
  exactly, 18.8048 percent, with a byte-identical route log and a
  byte-identical identity artifact, because the detector routes AC-PROSE
  to PROSE and PROSE is OB-1's original set unmodified. The
