0. WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS

OB-1 (research/OB1-EXPOSURE-1.md) showed that a MoE model can be served from a
BOUNDED resident expert set without changing a single output byte: the router's
misses are read from the GGUF on demand, digest-verified, used by the unmodified
kernel, and dropped again. It also showed the weakness of doing that with a
STATIC set. A set ranked by expert popularity on one prose route log missed
18.80 percent of router picks at K=16 on same-domain prose, but 60.57 percent on
out-of-domain code. The resident set was right about the wrong workload.

OB-2 asks the direct follow-on, in the prereg's words: can a DETERMINISTIC
DYNAMIC residency policy cut the miss rate at the same K, with identity
preserved? "Deterministic" is load-bearing and is defined by the prereg's
determinism wall: the resident set governing any router decision must be a pure
function of the route history strictly BEFORE the boundary that governs it, plus
fixed integer constants. Never of timing, never of cache state, never of a float.

Builder 1 simulated four candidate policies against OB-1's banked route logs and
froze the winner before any engine existed. This document is builder 2: the
engine that implements that frozen policy on the real model, and the runs that
test it.


1. THE FROZEN POLICY (prereg sections 3 and 7, not renegotiated here)

P2 DECAY-COUNTER. Per layer, one integer counter per expert, all starting at
zero. At each boundary b (a multiple of H=256 tokens) with b > 0:

    1. every counter is right-shifted by 1 (integer halving), reflecting all
       history strictly before b;
    2. the resident set for the window [b, b+256) is read off as the top K of
       those just-decayed counters, ranked by descending count then ascending
       expert id;
    3. the window is served;
    4. each pick served in that window adds 65536 to its expert's counter,
       which the NEXT boundary consumes.

Cold start (b=0): all counters are zero, so the top-K rule with its lower-id
tie-break yields the lowest K expert ids.

That is the whole policy. It is four integer operations and a sort of 32 items.


2. THE THREE BARS (prereg section 9, frozen before this engine existed)

  IDENTITY, stop-ship. Every leased run's identity artifact AND route log must
  be byte-identical (sha256) to OB-1's banked fully-resident reference for that
  corpus, at every K. One failure halts the leg. The point of a residency policy
  is that a miss is SERVED, never approximated.

  MISS RATE, report-only. The engine's measured per-pick miss rate must fall
  within 2.0 percentage points of the committed simulator's prediction for the
  same policy/corpus/K. A larger gap means the engine does not implement the
  frozen policy (a boundary off-by-one, a wrong constant, a non-causal leak) and
  is a bug to find, not a result to report.

  COST. Leased p95 must be <= 3.0x OB-1's own fully-resident baseline p95 for
  that corpus: prose 16025.8 ms, code 16073.7 ms (research/ob1/RUNLOG-1.txt).

5. WHAT THE ENGINE ACTUALLY CHANGES

OB-1's engine loaded a fixed resident set at model-load time and, at each route
callback, leased in whatever the router asked for that was not in it, dropping
those leases once the layer was computed. OB-2 keeps all of that machinery --
the same manifest, the same pread, the same sha256 verification, the same
madvise drop -- and makes the resident set itself move:

  - g_resident is no longer read from RESIDENT-SETS.json. It starts at the
    lowest K ids (the cold-start rule) and is recomputed at every H boundary
    from the integer counters described in section 1.
  - An expert that leaves the resident set is EVICTED: its pages are dropped
    from the pool with MADV_DONTNEED.
  - An expert that enters the resident set is PROMOTED: it leases in through the
    identical digest-verified path and then STAYS, instead of being dropped at
    the end of the layer.
  - ob1_on_route() now receives k and n_tokens separately rather than their
    product, because the policy counts boundaries in TOKENS and one ubatch spans
    several of them.

Kernels, tensors, graph node order and numerics are untouched. Only the
provenance and the lifetime of the expert bytes change.

A property of the measurement setup that the simulator cannot see, and that this
document reports rather than hides: BYTE MOVEMENT IS AT UBATCH GRANULARITY. The
runs use -b 1024 -ub 1024, so one mul_mat_id call computes 1024 tokens at once
and every expert that any of those 1024 tokens routes to must be in RAM
simultaneously. The engine therefore holds the union over that ubatch's four
policy windows of their resident sets, plus every expert the router actually
picked in the ubatch, and reports that union as ob2_physneed_*. Section 9 gives
the measured figure and what it means.

7. A DRY RUN OF THE ENGINE'S LOOP, BEFORE ANY MODEL PROCESS

The committed simulator walks one layer over all 32768 tokens. The engine cannot:
it is handed one ubatch of 1024 tokens per layer, in graph order, and must carry
per-layer policy state across callbacks and handle every boundary inside a
ubatch. That is a different loop shape over the same policy, and a different loop
shape is exactly where a boundary off-by-one lives.

research/ob2/engine_walk_check.py re-expresses the ENGINE's loop in Python --
ubatch-major, carried state, windows split at H boundaries inside a ubatch -- and
checks it against the committed simulator. It costs no runlock and no model run.
Run against the banked res-code-a route log at K=16, H=256, ubatch=1024:

    ENGINE-SHAPED WALK   misses=452228 picks=3145728 miss_pct=14.3759409587 churn=9116 boundaries=3072
    COMMITTED SIMULATOR  misses=452228 picks=3145728 miss_pct=14.3759409587 churn=9116
    AGREE=PASS (exact)

This is what made the live unit and the live matrix cheap: the loop was known
correct before the runlock was ever taken.

THE ANSWER TO OB-2's QUESTION IS YES ON THE MISS RATE AND NOT YET ON THE BYTES.

The policy cuts out-of-domain misses by three quarters and closes the
domain-transfer gap OB-1 found. At K=16 the static set was 3.2x worse on code
than on prose (60.57 against 18.80); the dynamic set is essentially domain
blind (14.38 against 14.93, so code is now marginally EASIER than prose). It
does that with identity intact and with reproducible, purely integer decisions.

But in these runs it moves the same bytes, and on prose very slightly more. The
reason is in the stats above and is not a defect in the policy: phys_mean is
30.1 to 31.4 experts out of 32. At -ub 1024 one mul_mat_id call computes 1024
tokens, and 1024 tokens route to nearly every expert in a layer no matter which
16 the policy calls resident. The resident set decides which of those loads are
FREE, but at this batch size almost all 32 must be in RAM regardless, so almost
all of them are paid for either way. OB-1 met the same wall from the other side
(its deviation D3).

Replaying the identical policy at ubatch=1, the shape a real decode has
(research/ob2/engine_walk_check.py, no model process; AGREE=PASS exact against
the committed simulator in all four cases):

  case          phys_max   phys_mean   expert-loads   static loads (= its misses)   ratio
  K=16 code      20 (K+4)    16.5750         456515                       1905471   4.17x fewer
  K=16 prose     20 (K+4)    16.5974         474711                        591548   1.25x fewer
  K=8  code      12 (K+4)     9.4553        1147145                       2508167   2.19x fewer
  K=8  prose     12 (K+4)     9.4706        1159785                       1331123   1.15x fewer

At decode granularity the bound is exactly K + 4 experts per layer, the resident
set plus the current token's own four picks, and the miss-rate win becomes a
byte-movement win of up to 4.17x. The miss rate is the decode-relevant number;
the matrix's batch-1024 byte columns are not, and are reported here so that
nobody reads a 76 percent miss cut as a 76 percent I/O cut on this evidence.

11. DEVIATIONS

D1  THREAD COUNT, AND WHAT IT DOES TO THE COST RATIO. OB-1's run-ob1.sh used
    --threads 10 --threads-batch 10. This leg used 8, the venue's stated cap of
    8 threads per heavy run. The cost limb therefore compares an 8-thread leased
    run against a 10-thread fully-resident baseline, which inflates this leg's
    ratio rather than flattering it: a thread-matched comparison could only move
    the numbers down. The bar passes at 1.42 to 1.65 against 3.0 either way. The
    absolute p95 figures are honest measurements of what this leg actually ran;
    the RATIOS are upper bounds.

D2  BYTE MOVEMENT IS AT UBATCH GRANULARITY, NOT PER TOKEN. Inherited from OB-1's
    deviation D3 and stated in full in sections 5 and 9. One mul_mat_id call
    computes 1024 tokens, so every expert any of them routes to must be resident
    simultaneously; the engine holds the union over the ubatch's policy windows
    plus everything routed, and reports it as ob2_physneed_*. Reducing -ub to 1
    would change the batching and therefore the very numbers being compared for
    identity, so it was not done live; the decode-granularity figures in section
    9 come from replaying the same policy over the banked route logs instead,
    and are labelled as such rather than presented as measured runs.

D3  madvise DROPS ONLY WHOLE INTERIOR PAGES. Inherited unchanged from OB-1's
    deviation D4 (the engine reuses ob1_drop). A per-expert slice is 4406400 or
    11520 bytes, neither a multiple of the 4096-byte page, so a slice's edge
    pages can be shared with a neighbour that may be resident and are left
    alone. Measured at K=16 on AC-CODE: 156407621760 bytes leased against
    155913744384 dropped, a shortfall of 493877376 bytes, 0.32 percent, over
    11801 lease events.

D4  A DRIVER BUG, FOUND BY ITS OWN EXIT CODE, BEFORE ANY MEASUREMENT. The first
    unit attempt passed the trace path with a conditional prefix,
    ${TRACE:+OB2_TRACE=$TRACE}. sh parses assignments in a command prefix
    grammatically, so an expansion producing "VAR=value" is taken as a COMMAND
    NAME. Verbatim failure, /root/ob2/runs/unit-k16-code/stderr.txt:

      /mnt/f/f32/openbob-wt/research-2/research/ob2/run-ob2.sh: 48:
      OB2_TRACE=/root/ob2/unit-trace.txt: not found

    with exit_rc 127, no model process started, no artifacts produced, and the
    runlock correctly acquired and released around the failure. Fixed by passing
    OB2_TRACE unconditionally (the engine treats an empty env value as unset)
    and by adding exit $RC to the driver so a failed run can no longer report
    rc=0 to its caller. No measurement was taken before or after the fix that
    the fix could have affected: the binary was untouched.

    per corpus). Over the full 32768-token runs the equality claim rests on the
    miss counts agreeing EXACTLY, five times, over 3145728 picks each: any
    divergence in the schedule at any of the 3072 boundaries would have to leave
    the total pick-by-pick miss count unchanged to hide, which is possible in
    principle and vanishingly unlikely in fact. Stated this way rather than as a
    byte-compare that was not performed. Capturing a full-run trace would have
    cost one more 14-minute leased run and is available if the architect wants
    the stronger form.

12. WHAT THIS LEG ANSWERS, AND WHAT IT HANDS ON

ANSWERED. A deterministic dynamic residency policy does cut the miss rate at the
same K, with identity preserved. P2 DECAY-COUNTER, four integer operations and a
32-item sort per boundary, cuts out-of-domain misses at K=16 from 60.5733 to
14.3759 percent, a 76.3 percent relative cut, and removes the domain-transfer
gap that was OB-1's headline weakness. Identity held byte for byte in all five
runs. The A/A pair is identical. The engine and the frozen model of it agree
exactly, on both the schedule and the count.

HANDED ON, three things the architect may want to weigh:

  1. The miss-rate win is not yet an I/O win at batch 1024, and the reason is
     structural, not a tuning problem. The decode-shape replay says the win is
     there at ubatch=1 (up to 4.17x fewer expert-loads, bound exactly K+4). A
     leg that measures a real decode path, rather than a perplexity batch, is
     what would turn this result into a memory result.

  2. Churn is cheap but not free: 9116 to 10394 symmetric-difference events per
     run at K=16, of which only about a fifth became promotions. H=256 was
     frozen from simulation and never tuned against live cost. If the decode
     leg finds promotion cost material, H and the shift are the two dials.

  3. The policy is domain blind by construction, which is the point, but it also
     means a cold start pays the full lowest-K-ids penalty. The unit's first
     4096 tokens missed 16.66 percent against the run-long 14.38 percent. A
     warm-start rule (seed the counters from a banked profile) is the obvious
     next candidate and would need its own prereg, since seeding from a profile
     reintroduces exactly the domain assumption this policy removed.
